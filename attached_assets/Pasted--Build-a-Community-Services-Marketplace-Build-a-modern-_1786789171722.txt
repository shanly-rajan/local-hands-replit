# Build a Community Services Marketplace

Build a modern, secure, community-friendly web application that connects local residents with trusted service providers.

The application should support **two complementary ways of finding help**:

1. Customers can search for and choose service providers.
2. Customers can post a problem together with their budget, allowing interested providers to express interest in doing the work.

This second model is important because it gives customers more control instead of making them completely dependent on provider pricing.

The application must make it easy to answer questions such as:

* Who is a reliable plumber nearby?
* Which electrician has been recommended by people in my community?
* Who offers good quality work at a fair price?
* Is this provider verified?
* What have previous customers experienced?
* Who offers the best overall value?
* Who is interested in doing my job within my budget?
* What services or businesses are advertising in my community?

The overall principle should be:

> **Trust, choice and value for the customer.**

---

# 1. Core Product Vision

Create a community services marketplace where residents can discover trusted providers or openly post work they need completed.

The platform should create a healthy balance between:

**Provider-led discovery**

> "I need a plumber. Show me trusted plumbers nearby."

and:

**Customer-led job discovery**

> "My kitchen tap is leaking. My budget is R600. Who is interested in doing the job?"

This gives customers more purchasing power while giving local service providers access to genuine job opportunities.

# 4. Budget Model

Allow the customer to specify:

### Fixed Budget

Example:

**Budget: R600**

or:

### Budget Range

Example:

**Budget: R500 – R800**

or:

### Open to Quotes

The customer does not yet know what the service should cost.

Clearly explain that budgets are generally **indicative until the provider understands the scope of work**.

Do not force providers to accept unrealistic budgets.

A provider should be able to respond:

> I'm interested and can work within your R600 budget.

or:

> I'm interested, but based on the description I estimate R750–R900. I would need to inspect the problem first.

This keeps customers empowered without encouraging misleading pricing.

---

# 5. Provider Expressions of Interest

Providers should not automatically receive a job.

Instead, they should **express interest**.

Each expression of interest should contain:

* Provider.
* Verification status.
* Overall rating.
* Community Value Score.
* Number of reviews.
* Short message.
* Proposed price or price range.
* Whether the customer's budget can be met.
* Estimated availability.
* Date submitted.

Example:

> **Thabo Plumbing Services**
>
> ✓ Verified
>
> ⭐ 4.8 from 67 reviews
>
> Community Value Score: 91/100
>
> "I can assist Saturday morning and the R600 budget should be sufficient assuming no replacement pipes are required."
>
> **Interested**

---

# 6. Customer Provider Selection

Once providers express interest, the customer should see a comparison screen.

For example:

| Provider          | Verified | Rating | Reviews | Value Score | Customer Budget | Provider Estimate |
| ----------------- | -------- | -----: | ------: | ----------: | --------------: | ----------------: |
| Thabo Plumbing    | ✓        |    4.8 |      67 |          91 |            R600 |              R600 |
| QuickFix Plumbing | ✓        |    4.6 |     121 |          89 |            R600 |         R550–R700 |
| Joe Handyman      | ✓        |    4.9 |      14 |          84 |            R600 |              R600 |

Allow the customer to open each provider's profile before selecting them.

The customer should retain control over who receives the work.

Do not automatically award jobs based purely on the lowest price.

# 8. Privacy of Customer Job Posts

Community jobs should protect customer privacy.

Public job listings may show:

* Approximate area/community.
* Job description.
* Service category.
* Budget.
* Photos selected by the customer.

Do **not** publicly expose:

* Exact home address.
* Personal phone number.
* Personal email address.
* Sensitive customer information.

Exact contact/address information should only become available when appropriate in the service interaction.

# 12. Provider Verification

Verification is a core trust mechanism.

Support verification for both individuals and companies.

Verification statuses:

```text
NOT_VERIFIED
PENDING
VERIFIED
REJECTED
SUSPENDED
```

Verification information may include:

### Individual

* Full name.
* Mobile number.
* Email.
* Identity document.
* Proof of address.
* Professional certification where appropriate.

### Company

* Company name.
* Registration number.
* Contact person.
* Business documentation.
* Address.
* Relevant professional/trade certificates.

Verification requirements may differ by country.

Design verification using configurable rules rather than assuming every country uses identical documentation.

Display:

**✓ Verified Provider**

on approved providers.

Verification documents must never be publicly accessible.

# 13. Reviews and Ratings

Only authenticated users should be able to review providers.

Where possible, prioritise reviews associated with an actual completed job.

Allow ratings for:

* Quality of work.
* Price fairness.
* Reliability.
* Professionalism.
* Overall experience.

Each should use a 1–5 rating.

Allow written reviews.

Allow providers to respond.

Distinguish reviews linked to a completed job with a badge such as:

**✓ Verified Job**

This gives those reviews greater credibility.

# 14. Community Value Score

Create a **Community Value Score from 0–100**.

The score should help users understand which providers offer strong overall value.

Consider:

* Quality: 30%
* Overall customer rating: 25%
* Price fairness: 20%
* Reliability: 15%
* Professionalism: 10%

Also incorporate review confidence.

For example:

```text
Provider A
4.9 stars
3 reviews

Provider B
4.7 stars
184 reviews
```

Provider B may deserve a stronger ranking because the rating is supported by considerably more evidence.

Do not allow a single five-star review to dominate the rankings.

Show:

> **Community Value Score: 91/100**

and provide a simple explanation:

> Excellent work quality, consistently strong reviews and fair pricing.

The scoring engine should remain modular so weighting can be adjusted later.

# 22. Technical Architecture

Choose a stack appropriate for Replit deployment.

Prefer:

## Backend

Python using:

* FastAPI, or
* Flask.

Choose the framework that gives the cleanest maintainable solution.

## Frontend

Prefer a lightweight frontend.

Use:

* Server-rendered templates.
* Bootstrap or Tailwind.
* Minimal JavaScript where appropriate.

Avoid introducing React unless the additional complexity genuinely benefits the application.

## Database

Use PostgreSQL.

SQLite may be used for local experimentation, but the production architecture should assume PostgreSQL.

Use an ORM.

# 28. Product Philosophy

The application should not simply be another business directory.

It should create a **two-sided community marketplace**.

Customers can say:

> "Show me a trusted electrician."

or:

> "I need these lights installed and I have R800 available. Who can help?"

Providers can say:

> "There are three electrical jobs available near me today."

The platform should facilitate the connection while using **verification, reputation, transparency and community feedback** to create trust.

The design philosophy is:

> **The customer has choice.
> The provider has opportunity.
> The community creates trust.**

The application should feel local, safe, inclusive and extremely easy to use.

Think:

> **"My neighbourhood knows who does good work, and now I can find them."**
